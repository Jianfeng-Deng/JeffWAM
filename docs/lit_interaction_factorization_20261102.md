# Interaction factorization for a manipulation world model

Literature survey, 2 November 2026. Scope: one overhead RGB camera; fine-tuned DINOv2-B 12×20 patch tokens and up to nine SAM3-mask object tokens; 84 dual-arm bottle-to-bin teleoperation demonstrations, 15 Hz, 10-step joint-action chunks. No forward kinematics, calibration or hand-written object-specific transition rules as model inputs. The diagnostics are (A) one demonstrated action per state and weak identification of alternatives, (B) action swaps cause excessive changes everywhere, including the bin, and (C) object predictions are worse than copying the current token.

## Executive assessment

Factorization is a good **inductive bias**, not a discovery guarantee. Keep distinct robot, object and scene representations, but let an object switch from passive to action-influenced as interactions occur. A bottle in hand is not globally uncontrollable, and a fixed bin matters as a goal and a possible collision partner. Use an identity/residual prediction path and a **soft**, state-conditioned gate for action-dependent residuals; maintain a nonzero loss on all valid tokens and explicitly upweight observed motion/contact. Predicting physical object state and predicting its context-dependent DINO appearance are different tasks: a stationary bin can change its token because of occlusion, shadows or ViT context mixing.

Most useful immediate combination: reliable tokens and motion-stratified copy baselines; a residual predictor with a small action-independent pathway and gated robot-to-object messages; then a small, purpose-collected set of repeated or matched initial conditions with varied safe actions. Test action sensitivity on real outcomes, not only latent swaps. Point tracks supply useful cross-embodiment motion supervision, but neither a contact probability nor MI between two latents proves causal influence. A high mutual information between hand and object can arise from common background, correlated trajectories or masked-encoder leakage. In fact, maximizing unconditional MI can actively encourage these shortcuts.

**Reading the entries.** Each includes mechanism and interaction signal, empirical setting/scale where available, and applicability. “No contact labels” does not mean no other supervision: tracking, segmentation, actions, reward, and simulation states may be supervised or generated. Data sizes from papers are not directly comparable to 84 correlated demonstrations. All bibliographic links resolve to the cited paper or its proceedings; where the publication year differs from the arXiv posting year, the venue year is used. ArXiv IDs are supplied where confirmed; a direct proceedings URL is provided otherwise.

## 1. Controllable, relevant and robot-aware representations

1. **Iso-Dream: Isolating and Leveraging Noncontrollable Visual Dynamics in World Models**, Minting Pan, Xiangming Zhu, Yunbo Wang, Xiaokang Yang, NeurIPS 2022, [arXiv:2205.13817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13817). Separate controllable and noncontrollable latent branches; inverse-action prediction encourages separation and the latter is rolled out for planning. It learns from action-labeled trajectories rather than interaction labels; action variation matters. Tested on visual-control and driving domains, not 84 real manipulation demonstrations. Transfer the branch structure to DINO/SAM, not the assumption that passive objects remain independent during contact.

2. **Denoised MDPs: Learning World Models Better Than the World Itself**, Tongzhou Wang, Simon S. Du, Antonio Torralba, Phillip Isola, Amy Zhang, Yuandong Tian, ICML 2022, [arXiv:2206.15477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15477). Factorizes dynamics by controllability and reward relevance, using a structured latent generative model and reward signal. Evaluated on distractor variants of DeepMind Control and RoboDesk; released configurations use up to 10^6 environment steps. Conceptually useful, but rewards, large interactive data and pixel reconstruction are extra assumptions; DINO slots can implement a smaller analogue.

3. **Learning Task Informed Abstractions**, Xiang Fu, Ge Yang, Pulkit Agrawal, Tommi Jaakkola, ICML 2021, [arXiv:2106.15612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.15612). Two jointly reconstructing branches are split by reward prediction and adversarially suppress reward information in the distractor branch; this is reward relevance, not physical contact detection. Tested in ManyWorld, visual distractor control, and Atari. A warning for our goal: a bin may be action-insensitive but still important; sparse or absent reward makes direct use difficult.

4. **SeMAIL: Eliminating Distractors in Visual Imitation via Separated Models**, Shenghua Wan, Yucen Wang, Minghao Shao, Ruying Chen, De-Chuan Zhan, ICML 2023, [arXiv:2306.10695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10695). An action-conditioned relevant branch and action-independent irrelevant branch reconstruct observations and support adversarial imitation; there are no contact labels. Expert and agent buffers on visual-control tasks with changing distractors provide data, not an offline 84-demo identification result. A useful separation pattern for slots, but a hard action-independent object branch would fail on grasping.

5. **Information Prioritization through Empowerment in Visual Model-based RL (InfoPower)**, Homanga Bharadhwaj, Mohammad Babaeizadeh, Dumitru Erhan, Sergey Levine, ICLR 2022, [arXiv:2204.08585](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08585). Uses a variational inverse-action/empowerment objective in a reconstruction-free world model to emphasize action-correlated features; not a pairwise contact estimator. In simulated robotic control with moving visual distractors it reports results at 100k–1M interactions. Attractive for DINO latents, but action-prediction shortcuts through the directly moving arm or demonstration phase are likely with 84 demos.

6. **Know Thyself: Transferable Visual Control Policies Through Robot-Awareness**, Edward S. Hu, Kun Huang, Oleh Rybkin, Dinesh Jayaraman, ICLR 2022 Generalizable Policy Learning in the Physical World workshop, [arXiv:2107.09047](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.09047). Separates robot and world and combines a world model with known robot dynamics and projected robot masks. Cross-robot simulated and real tabletop manipulation shows transfer; the paper explicitly uses robot state, morphology and camera calibration. Architectural inspiration only: substitute learned visual robot tokens, since its original FK/calibration interface violates our constraint.

7. **Learning to Poke by Poking: Experiential Learning of Intuitive Physics**, Pulkit Agrawal, Ashvin Nair, Pieter Abbeel, Jitendra Malik, Sergey Levine, NeurIPS 2016, [arXiv:1606.07419](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07419). Joint forward and inverse prediction makes visual features useful for action and object displacement, without object-state labels. Real robot data comprises over 100,000 pokes and 400 hours, with varied poke locations/directions. Demonstrates the action-diversity gap: an inverse auxiliary loss is cheap to add to DINO, but its experimental support comes from orders of magnitude more varied physical interactions.

## 2. Local causal influence, learned dependencies and graphs

8. **Causal Influence Detection for Improving Efficiency in Reinforcement Learning**, Maximilian Seitzer, Bernhard Schölkopf, Georg Martius, NeurIPS 2021, [arXiv:2106.03443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03443). CAI quantifies situation-dependent influence by conditional mutual information between an action and a target entity's next state given the current state, estimated from learned conditional dynamics and an action distribution. It requires no contact labels, but needs a meaningful conditional action distribution and trained alternatives. Demonstrated for exploration in factored simulated settings, not established for one-action-per-state visual demos; direct application would mostly measure model extrapolation.

9. **Counterfactual Data Augmentation using Locally Factored Dynamics (CoDA)**, Silviu Pitis, Elliot Creager, Animesh Garg, NeurIPS 2020, [arXiv:2007.02863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.02863). Given known or learned local dependency masks, recombines disconnected transition components and checks their local factorization. Evaluated on sprite and simulated multi-object manipulation/offline RL, with both specified and learned structure. Object-factorized SAM tokens suggest a route, but naive swapping of an arm, bottle or background can violate contact, image geometry or scene support; use only as a verified later ablation.

10. **MoCoDA: Model-based Counterfactual Data Augmentation**, Silviu Pitis, Elliot Creager, Ajay Mandlekar, Animesh Garg, NeurIPS 2022, [arXiv:2210.11287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11287). Uses a locally factored learned transition model to synthesize transitions for an augmented state-action distribution; theoretical generalization relies on local structure and coverage assumptions. Evaluated on navigation and offline simulated robotic manipulation. Compatible with factored embeddings in principle, but synthetic actions do not create information missing from the 84 demonstrations and model errors become training labels.

11. **Causal Dynamics Learning for Task-Independent State Abstraction**, Zizhao Wang, Xuesu Xiao, Zifan Xu, Yuke Zhu, Peter Stone, ICML 2022, [arXiv:2206.13452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13452). Learns sparse state/action parents by conditional dependence and uses the learned causal dynamics to derive task-independent abstractions. Two simulated environments and downstream tasks test generalization. It assumes suitable factored state variables and sufficient coverage; masks supply candidate factors but high-dimensional entangled DINO tokens and deterministic teleoperation challenge the causal interpretation.

12. **ELDEN: Exploration via Local Dependencies**, Zizhao Wang, Jiaheng Hu, Peter Stone, Roberto Martín-Martín, NeurIPS 2023, [arXiv:2310.08702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08702). Derivatives of learned dynamics estimate context-dependent entity dependencies; disagreement across an ensemble rewards exploration of uncertain relations. Four 2D and 3D simulated domains test chained interactions. Derivatives in our model are cheap diagnostics, not causal proof; useful data acquisition is blocked without real interventions, and high-dimensional Jacobian regularization can be costly.

13. **Generalizing Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning with Variational Causal Reasoning (GRADER)**, Wenhao Ding, Haohong Lin, Bo Li, Ding Zhao, NeurIPS 2022, [arXiv:2207.09081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09081). Alternates inference of a latent causal graph from interventional experience with goal-conditioned dynamics/policy learning. Evaluated on nine tasks including simulated stacking and causal graph discovery. Demonstrates the value of actual interventions; directly porting graph discovery from low-dimensional factors to entangled visual tokens with no action overlap would be unjustified.

14. **Fine-Grained Causal Dynamics Learning with Quantization for Improving Robustness in Reinforcement Learning**, Inwoo Hwang, Yunhyeok Kwak, Suhyung Choi, Byoung-Tak Zhang, Sanghack Lee, ICML 2024, [arXiv:2406.03234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03234). A learned discrete context partitions state-action space into subgroups with different sparse dependencies, trained jointly with prediction; no manually annotated contact graph. Simulated generalization under locally spurious correlations supports context-dependent, rather than global, edges. A small gating network on mask tokens is plausible; reliable subgroups cannot be expected from 84 near-identical trajectories alone.

15. **SPARTAN: A Sparse Transformer World Model Attending to What Matters**, Anson Lei, Bernhard Schölkopf, Ingmar Posner, NeurIPS 2025, [arXiv:2411.06890](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06890). Regularizes attention among object-factored tokens to yield sparse, context-dependent interaction structure, and studies adaptation to sparse interventions. Evaluated in object-centric observation-based environments and distractor/intervention tests. Easy architectural match to SAM/DINO; attention is a learned routing proxy, not guaranteed causal attribution, and sparsity can suppress rare contacts.

16. **Neural Relational Inference for Interacting Systems**, Thomas Kipf, Ethan Fetaya, Kuan-Chieh Wang, Max Welling, Richard Zemel, ICML 2018, [arXiv:1802.04687](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.04687). Infers a latent interaction graph jointly with graph-network dynamics from sequences without graph labels. Demonstrated on simulated physical systems and real motion-capture/sports tracks, mostly with observed entity trajectories. Graph inference is a useful template for hand–object embeddings, but inferred correlations need variation and need not identify interventions; original settings do not establish real robot contact learning.

17. **Interaction Networks for Learning about Objects, Relations and Physics**, Peter W. Battaglia, Razvan Pascanu, Matthew Lai, Danilo Rezende, Koray Kavukcuoglu, NeurIPS 2016, [arXiv:1612.00222](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00222). Shared pairwise message functions and aggregation predict each entity's dynamics on a supplied graph. Tested on simulated n-body, collisions and non-rigid dynamics, with trajectories of dozens of objects. This is a relational architecture, not automatic contact detection; use small shared message functions over SAM tokens, with a learned gate and no object-specific rule.

## 3. Object-centric, slot and particle world models

18. **Contrastive Learning of Structured World Models**, Thomas Kipf, Elise van der Pol, Max Welling, ICLR 2020, [arXiv:1911.12247](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.12247). C-SWM discovers object-like slots and predicts action-conditioned graph dynamics with contrastive latent objectives, without pixel reconstruction or object labels. Tested on structured grid environments, Atari and three-body simulation. Fits JEPA-style token prediction well; slot/object alignment and direct action effects still require auditing, especially with similar bottles.

19. **SlotFormer: Unsupervised Visual Dynamics Simulation with Object-Centric Models**, Ziyi Wu, Nikita Dvornik, Klaus Greff, Thomas Kipf, Animesh Garg, ICLR 2023, [paper and project](https://slotformer.github.io/). Transformer predicts temporal slot sequences and their interactions, without interaction labels; planning is evaluated as a downstream use. Demonstrated on object-centric video and simulated physical reasoning, not the same as joint-command robot control. Temporal slot histories are directly applicable to mask-token stability; action conditioning and actual manipulation identification need to be added.

20. **Generative Video Transformer: Can Objects be the Words? (OCVT)**, Yi-Fu Wu, Jaesik Yoon, Sungjin Ahn, ICML 2021, [arXiv:2107.09240](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.09240). Autoregressively predicts object tokens and uses matching to maintain object correspondence. Unsupervised video prediction and CATER reasoning test object interaction; 70-frame training fits one 48 GB GPU in the paper. Useful identity/temporal matching idea, but original video prediction does not identify action effects and its learned object locations differ from SAM masks.

21. **SOLD: Slot Object-Centric Latent Dynamics Models for Relational Manipulation Learning from Pixels**, Malte Mosbach, Jan Niklas Ewertz, Angel Villar-Corrales, Sven Behnke, ICML 2025, [arXiv:2410.08822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08822). Learns unsupervised object-centric slots and a latent dynamics model for model-based RL; action-conditioned experience trains interaction implicitly. Tested across simulated relational robotic manipulation benchmarks with online learning. Structurally relevant, but do not infer similar sample efficiency on 84 offline real demos; retaining SAM slots avoids training segmentation from scratch.

22. **FOCUS: object-centric world models for robotic manipulation**, Stefano Ferraro, Pietro Mazzaglia, Tim Verbelen, Bart Dhoedt, Frontiers in Neurorobotics 2025, [arXiv:2307.02427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.02427). Object-conditioned masked reconstruction yields object latents; object-entropy exploration encourages new interactions, without contact annotations. Manipulation experiments use up to 2M environment steps, with advantages appearing after roughly 500k, and proprioception enters its original model. SAM token extraction and object-balanced loss are applicable, but the original exploration/proprioception setup exceeds our constraints.

23. **Dreamweaver: Learning Compositional World Models from Pixels**, Junyeob Baek, Yi-Fu Wu, Gautam Singh, Sungjin Ahn, ICLR 2025, [arXiv:2501.14174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14174). Recurrent block-slot units decompose objects into reusable concepts; future prediction helps separate static and dynamic attributes from unlabeled videos. Evaluated for disentanglement and compositional video generation, not robot action intervention. Suggests separating persistent identity from motion state, but training this full model from 84 demonstrations is high risk.

24. **DINO-WM: World Models on Pre-trained Visual Features enable Zero-shot Planning**, Gaoyue Zhou, Hengkai Pan, Yann LeCun, Lerrel Pinto, ICML 2025, [arXiv:2411.04983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04983). Predicts action-conditioned DINOv2 patch features from offline trajectories, then optimizes actions toward visual goals. Evaluated in six environments including pushing, reacher and granular/rope tasks; training data include broad task-agnostic trajectories. Most direct JEPA comparison, but its patch-level success does not establish a contact graph or verify static-object invariance in our dataset.

25. **Slot Structured World Models**, Jonathan Collu, Riccardo Majellaro, Aske Plaat, Thomas M. Moerland, 2024 preprint, [arXiv:2402.03326](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03326). Combines Slot Attention with graph dynamics for action-conditioned object interactions. Spriteworld multi-step interaction tests favor the structured model, but are simple synthetic scenes. Swapping its encoder for SAM-derived tokens is plausible; slot consistency and contact coverage, not graph size, are the likely bottlenecks here.

26. **Learning to Simulate Complex Physics with Graph Networks (GNS)**, Alvaro Sanchez-Gonzalez, Jonathan Godwin, Tobias Pfaff, Rex Ying, Jure Leskovec, Peter W. Battaglia, ICML 2020, [arXiv:2002.09405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09405). Particle nodes and neighborhood message passing impose locality, while transition targets train dynamics. Synthetic materials include fluids, rigid and deformable solids; models extrapolate to many more particles and thousands of steps. Valuable message-passing bias, but metric particle states, neighborhood construction and simulator-scale trajectories are far from monocular DINO tokens; 2D adjacency is only a proxy.

27. **Learning Particle Dynamics for Manipulating Rigid Bodies, Deformable Objects, and Fluids (DPI-Net)**, Yunzhu Li, Jiajun Wu, Russ Tedrake, Joshua B. Tenenbaum, Antonio Torralba, ICLR 2019, [arXiv:1810.01566](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.01566). Dynamic particle interaction graphs and material-aware structures learn dynamics from trajectories; graphs supply physical locality, with different treatments for materials. Simulated and real fluid/deformable manipulation illustrate control. The principle transfers but the 3D particle pipeline and material assumptions are not available in our single-view system.

28. **OA-WAM: Object-Addressable World Action Model for Robust Robot Manipulation**, Yushan Liu, Peibo Sun, Shoujie Li, Yifan Xie, Lingfeng Zhang, Xintao Chao, Shiyuan Dong, Fang Chen, Xiao-Ping Zhang, Wenbo Ding, 2026 preprint, [arXiv:2605.06481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06481). Separates slot address from content and routes attention through persistent addresses while jointly predicting action chunks and future slots. Reports LIBERO and SimplerEnv simulation and a slot-swap binding test; it also uses language and proprioception. Slot identity/content separation is relevant, but its full action model and data scale are not a drop-in for our constraints; simulation and preprint evidence should be treated accordingly.

## 4. Persistence, sparse change and temporal preservation

29. **Unsupervised Learning for Physical Interaction through Video Prediction**, Chelsea Finn, Ian Goodfellow, Sergey Levine, NeurIPS 2016, [arXiv:1605.07157](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.07157). Action-conditioned DNA/CDNA predicts transformations and masks combining moved pixels with a static-background copy channel, trained on robot video without object labels. Real robot pushing evaluates motion and unseen objects. Direct precedent for a copy-versus-change pathway; pixel masks are not causal and visual copying alone fails under occlusion or moving camera.

30. **Self-Supervised Visual Planning with Temporal Skip Connections**, Frederik Ebert, Chelsea Finn, Alex X. Lee, Sergey Levine, CoRL 2017, [arXiv:1710.05268](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.05268). Action-conditioned video prediction copies/transforms pixels from multiple previous frames so information can survive occlusion; transformations and masks are learned from video. Real robot pushing used roughly 44,000 trajectories and tested unseen/multiple objects and obstacles. For us, use token history/visibility confidence, not a direct promise of reliable recovery after long occlusion.

31. **EVEWorld: Physical Evolution Supervision for Embodied World Models**, Kaiqi Wang, Songxin Zhang, Zejian Xie, Xiao Xiong, Zhuoyang Song, Ziwei Wu, Jun Yu Lu, Yitan Teng, Ziying Song, Jiaxing Zhang, 2026 preprint, [arXiv:2610.03374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03374). Uses instance-guided restoration and temporal instance alignment to penalize inconsistent object evolution in generated video. Evaluated on video-world-model benchmarks and instance consistency, not paired counterfactual robot actions. Instance consistency motivates visibility-aware object losses, but its large video model and generated-video metrics are not a small-data causal solution.

## 5. Hand–object contact, trajectories and cross-embodiment video

32. **Track2Act: Predicting Point Tracks from Internet Videos enables Generalizable Robot Manipulation**, Homanga Bharadhwaj, Roozbeh Mottaghi, Abhinav Gupta, Shubham Tulsiani, ECCV 2024, [arXiv:2405.01527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01527). Web-video-trained goal-conditioned 2D point-track forecasts describe object motion; a robot-side mapping and small residual policy execute plans. Reports more than 30 real-world tasks with generalization across scenes and objects. Tracks are embodiment-neutral observations and compatible as auxiliary targets for SAM regions, but action execution still involves robot-specific control; tracks are not contact-force labels.

33. **Any-point Trajectory Modeling for Policy Learning (ATM)**, Chuan Wen, Xingyu Lin, John So, Kai Chen, Qi Dou, Yang Gao, Pieter Abbeel, RSS 2024, [arXiv:2401.00025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00025). Pretrains future arbitrary-point trajectories from action-free video, then conditions a policy on predicted tracks using action-labeled robot demonstrations. Evaluated on over 130 language-conditioned simulated and real tasks, including cross-human/cross-robot video transfer. Offers useful trajectory supervision, not causal action attribution; external video and tracker quality are major data assumptions.

34. **Flow as the Cross-Domain Manipulation Interface (Im2Flow2Act)**, Mengda Xu, Zhenjia Xu, Yinghao Xu, Cheng Chi, Gordon Wetzstein, Manuela Veloso, Shuran Song, CoRL 2024, [arXiv:2407.15208](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.15208). Learns task-conditioned object flow from human video and a flow-to-action policy from simulated robot play; point tracks and moving masks are automatic pseudo-labels. Tests real rigid, articulated and deformable manipulation without real robot training data. Flow can supervise our object motion without FK, but simulation-trained action mapping is embodiment-specific and human-object co-motion is not an intervention.

35. **Grounded Human-Object Interaction Hotspots from Video**, Tushar Nagarajan, Christoph Feichtenhofer, Kristen Grauman, ICCV 2019, [arXiv:1812.04558](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.04558). Distills action anticipation into spatial hotspot maps on resting objects. OPRA and EPIC-Kitchens videos provide weak action/object category labels, rather than contact masks; novel-category affordance evaluation is reported. Good example of a learned soft interaction prior, but human egocentric/third-person transfer to top-view robot hands is unproven and the method is not label-free.

36. **Human Hands as Probes for Interactive Object Understanding**, Mohit Goyal, Sahil Modi, Rishabh Goyal, Saurabh Gupta, CVPR 2022, [arXiv:2112.09120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09120). Uses detected hands in egocentric videos to localize interactions and derive state-sensitive features and affordance/grasp regions from observed behavior. EPIC-KITCHENS supplies diverse human interactions; there are no manually annotated affordance maps in the proposed training signal, but hand detection brings pretrained supervision. A useful pseudo-label idea with masks and track histories, though hand appearance, top view, occlusion and embodiment transfer are open issues.

## Synthesis: what is and is not learned

**Data-driven structure.** NRI, ELDEN, CDL, FCDL, SPARTAN, CAI and CoDA's learned-mask variant infer dependency proxies from prediction or statistical variation rather than a hand-coded bottle rule. SlotFormer/C-SWM/SOLD learn representations or routing, not necessarily a causal graph. Track2Act/ATM/Im2Flow2Act use automatically tracked motion; hotspot and hand-probe work uses action labels or pretrained detectors as specified above. All embed inductive biases—sparsity, graph structure, temporal consistency or tracking—so none is assumption-free. Geometry is a soft prior, not a per-object rule: use distances and co-motion between *all* detected entities, not a designated bottle-to-hand edge.

**Conditional mutual information and one action per state.** Formally, CAI resembles I(A; O_{t+h} | S_t=s), averaged under a specified action distribution. If the behavior policy is deterministic given a sufficiently complete state, H(A|S)=0 and the observational conditional MI is zero; there is no conditional support for comparing alternatives. With continuous states, exact states rarely repeat, but estimation can still work by pooling smooth, sufficiently overlapping neighborhoods across episodes if actions vary after conditioning on all relevant confounders. Eighty-four demonstrations may contain some overlap; count it and test it rather than assert none. Hidden velocity, grasp state, operator intent and history create confounding/partial observability. An alternative-action neural rollout is a model-based hypothesis outside support, not an observed counterfactual; artificial action shuffles with the original future are false labels. Observational CMI equals intervention influence only under suitable consistency, conditional exchangeability/no hidden confounding and positivity, plus reliable representation and transition modeling. Randomized matched-state actions or defensible quasi-experiments can supply the missing support. Conditioning on a lossy token or changing the action distribution changes the estimand.

**Time and contact.** Ten frames at 15 Hz span about 0.67 s, so action-to-object influence can be delayed, indirect or occur mid-chunk. Estimate influence at multiple horizons and condition on recent history. An object's proximity to a hand is neither necessary nor sufficient for influence: a held object can move with a hand, push another object, or be dropped; top-view overlap can hide depth. Don't freeze bin tokens unconditionally: bin contact, occlusion and illumination can alter their observation, even if its physical pose stays fixed. Likewise, separate action-free evolution from action effects: a previously moving object may continue after the robot moves away.

**Failure modes.** (1) Slot collapse, duplicate slots, mask flicker, ID swaps and absent/occluded objects corrupt next-token targets; compare persistent IDs and visibility, use masked matching. (2) ViT features pool global context, so a stationary masked bin token can vary when a hand moves elsewhere; re-encode masked crops and compare frozen/fine-tuned teachers, padding and foreground/background perturbations. (3) At grasp, a pooled object mask can contain hand and object, and an arm token may include bottle pixels; appearance correlation is mistaken for influence. Inspect masks, erosion/overlap and patch receptive fields; use uncertain/overlap labels and history. (4) A zero-residual gate, static-class imbalance or aggressive sparsity beats average MSE while missing the rare grasp; balance motion and event phases and report copy-relative metrics. (5) A powerful global pathway can route action leakage around the gate; constrain and measure it, including action Jacobians. (6) Inverse-action heads can decode arm appearance or demo phase without explaining object response. (7) Overlapping temporal windows and near-duplicate demonstrations cause train/test leakage; split by full trajectory and collect held-out configurations. (8) Occlusion and 2D motion are not physical state changes; retain a visibility-aware belief and evaluate visible and occluded subsets separately.

## Five ranked design options

Costs are rough engineering estimates for one experienced researcher with the existing training pipeline and a single available GPU; robot collection and training wall time vary substantially. They are proposed experiments, not measured claims. A/B/C refer to the initial diagnoses. Do not introduce FK or calibration as model inputs in any option.

1. **Repair targets and establish motion-stratified baselines** — *1–3 engineering days, low compute; addresses B and C and measures A.* Freeze a teacher checkpoint during each experiment; audit timestamp alignment (chunk at t predicts exactly t+10, not t+9 or t+1), token ordering, SAM mask/ID validity, normalization, padding and loss scale. Compare copying, a constant-velocity token baseline from history, an action-free predictor and the current model on identical teacher features. Report per-object/patch MSE and cosine, normalized by copy error, separately for moving vs stationary, approach/grasp/carry/release, visible vs occluded and horizons 1/5/10. Use trajectory-level bootstrap intervals and held-out demonstrations. Plot actual versus swapped-action change for each token, but label the latter as sensitivity, not accuracy. This is a prerequisite for interpreting any architectural gain.

2. **Identity plus shared gated residual with event-balanced training** — *3–7 days, modest compute; addresses B and C, partially A.* For each token predict `z_hat_i = z_i + d_free_i(history) + g_i(history, action) * d_act_i(history, action)`. Use shared parameters across objects, a small robot-to-object attention/message block, bounded gate and weak sparsity penalty on action-dependent change; allow action-free dynamics and use a persistent background path. Start with a soft gate, not a distance threshold. Train with stop-gradient/frozen or EMA targets, nonzero baseline weight per valid token, and bounded motion/event weights derived offline from observed tracks or target deltas; normalize weights per batch and prevent gradients through weights. Compare plain residual, gate without sparsity, and gated/sparse against copy and action-free baselines. Evaluate static-bin action sensitivity and moving-bottle error together; reject a solution that fixes B by suppressing actual motion.

3. **Acquire a small matched-state action-diversity set** — *2–5 days setup plus robot/operator time; addresses A and validates B/C.* From reproducibly reset or carefully matched visual states, record several safe different action chunks, including a verified hold/no-op where feasible, approach variations, contact and post-contact motions; log actual executed action and synchronized frames. Use the same camera and action interface, no kinematics as model input. Split by reset/configuration and reserve entire action branches for evaluation. Compare observed and predicted per-object pairwise outcome differences at matched starts, with confidence intervals and separately no-contact/contact. If resets are imperfect, report visual-state mismatch and condition on history; do not call them exact interventions. Pilot a few repeated starts before scaling; coverage matters more than an arbitrary episode count. This is the most direct remedy for A, but ranked after cheap diagnostics because it requires physical collection.

4. **Visibility-aware object motion and interaction auxiliary targets** — *4–10 days plus offline tracker inference; addresses C and B, indirectly A.* Track multiple points inside each SAM mask, propagate IDs, estimate robust within-mask 2D motion and track confidence/occlusion. Predict future displacement or a distribution over tracks alongside latent residuals, using only past frames at inference; use co-motion and uncertain mask overlap as weak features for a shared interaction gate. Downweight unreliable tracks, avoid selecting moving regions solely from the target at test time, and never equate co-motion with causation. Freeze a pretrained tracker initially. Compare auxiliary motion-head and no-head versions on held-out moving objects, with physical image-space displacement metrics and latent copy-relative error. Cross-embodiment video pretraining is optional, not assumed to fit in 84 demonstrations.

5. **Learn sparse relations and test interventions, then consider cautious augmentation** — *1–3 weeks, moderate compute plus option 3 data; targets A and B, may improve C.* Add a shared pairwise graph gate or sparse attention over robot/object/scene tokens and compare a modest ELDEN-style finite-difference sensitivity/ensemble to actual held-out matched-action effects. Train on observed transitions only first; evaluate edge stability under token masking, background changes, different seeds and held-out intervention outcomes. If genuine local independence is supported, try CoDA-style recombination of independent components with visibility, plausibility and contact checks, compare against no augmentation, and never paste unverified action/outcome pairs.

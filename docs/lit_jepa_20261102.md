# JEPA and latent world models for dual-arm manipulation

Literature survey, search cutoff **2 November 2026**. Papers released later in November are necessarily outside the cutoff. The paper links below lead to the primary paper or its official proceedings record; titles and author attribution are checked against those records. “Preprint” means no publication venue is asserted here; it does not imply rejection. For space, “et al.” means the remaining authors are listed at the linked primary record. Recent preprint claims should be treated as provisional, not independent replications. The selection is not an exhaustive census.

## Decision for the present system

**Do not assume that the 240 spatial tokens are wasted, or that JEPA automatically removes nuisance detail.** The immediate opportunity is to measure and rebalance the supervision, preserve fine spatial evidence where contact needs it, and demand that predictions discriminate the consequences of actions. DINO-WM succeeds with spatial features; V-JEPA 2.1 reports substantially improved real-robot grasping with more spatially grounded dense features. Conversely, CF-JEPA finds that reconstruction-free latents can still retain distractors; physically grounded JEPA and physical-laziness studies find non-collapsed embeddings that omit useful state. These results do not establish the best token count for your task. [DINO-WM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04983), [V-JEPA 2.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14482), [CF-JEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00727), [physical grounding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03565), [physical laziness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04264).

Your 336×560 image has 24×40 DINOv2-B features, pooled 2×2 into 12×20 = 240 spatial tokens; these are a spatial grid, not 240 independent global summaries. Up to nine SAM3-mask object tokens coexist with them. At 15 Hz, +5/+10 steps are approximately 0.33/0.67 s; 3–6 ten-step chunks span 2–4 s. The useful question is whether each target helps distinguish **approach, contact, grasp, lift, carry, release and bottle-in-bin** outcomes, including both arms and occlusion. Physical laws cannot generally be inferred or identified from 84 near-successful, correlated demonstrations alone. A model that predicts contact geometry, object motion and action effects reliably within the demonstrated region may be much more valuable than a larger model claiming general physical reasoning.

## Reading the entries

Each numbered work gives mechanism and prediction granularity; anti-collapse or source of diversity; whether actions condition the predictor; evidence and relevance. **Action: no** includes action-free pretraining; it does not mean no downstream control. “Not applicable” means the work is a benchmark or theoretical analysis rather than an end-to-end JEPA training recipe. Where a paper's exact anti-collapse mechanism or training scale was not established from the available primary record, it is stated as such rather than guessed. Years below refer to initial paper year; venue is identified when verified, otherwise preprint.

### Foundations, theory and recent variants

1. **I-JEPA — “Self-Supervised Learning from Images with a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture.”** Mahmoud Assran et al., 2023, CVPR 2023, [arXiv:2301.08243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08243). Predicts target **spatial block features** from informative visible context; large target blocks and distributed context matter. EMA target and stop-gradient stabilize training; action: no. ImageNet ViT-H trained on 16 A100s; image representation evaluation, not dynamics. **Us:** masking and feature targets are design choices, not a proof that every token is semantic.

2. **V-JEPA — “Revisiting Feature Prediction for Learning Visual Representations from Video.”** Adrien Bardes et al., 2024, NeurIPS 2024, [arXiv:2404.08471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08471). Predicts masked **spatiotemporal token features**, with EMA target, no negatives or pixel reconstruction; action: no. Trained on 2 million public videos; frozen features do well on motion and appearance benchmarks. **Us:** temporal masking supplies motion supervision, but this is not an action-intervention study.

3. **MC-JEPA — “A Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture for Self-Supervised Learning of Motion and Content Features.”** Adrien Bardes, Jean Ponce, Yann LeCun, 2023, preprint, [arXiv:2307.12698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12698). Joint content features and optical-flow estimation in a shared encoder; dense motion plus content, action: no. JEPA training uses a target branch; optical flow also supplies a distinct training signal. Evaluated on optical flow and semantic segmentation. **Us:** explicitly probe motion instead of presuming semantic image features encode it.

4. **V-JEPA 2 / V-JEPA 2-AC — “Self-Supervised Video Models Enable Understanding, Prediction and Planning.”** Mido Assran et al., 2025, preprint, [arXiv:2506.09985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985). Action-free masked video feature prediction followed by an **action-conditioned** autoregressive spatial-token predictor and image-goal planning. Pretraining uses EMA target; AC leverages the pretrained representation. Over one million hours of internet video plus under 62 hours of DROID robot video for AC; zero-shot Franka pick/place in two labs. **Us:** important planning evidence, but the robot adaptation inherits massive pretraining; 62 hours is not the total learning budget.

5. **V-JEPA 2.1 — “Unlocking Dense Features in Video Self-Supervised Learning.”** Lorenzo Mur-Labadia et al., 2026, preprint, [arXiv:2603.14482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14482). Dense visible-and-masked token prediction, intermediate-layer supervision and image/video tokenizers; pretrained JEPA target machinery. Action-free representation learning, followed by robot adaptation. Reports a 20 percentage-point gain in real robot grasping success over V-JEPA 2-AC, plus dense spatial benchmarks. **Us:** spatial detail can be control-relevant; preserve resolution near hands and object, not necessarily everywhere.

6. **LeJEPA — “Provable and Scalable Self-Supervised Learning Without the Heuristics.”** Randall Balestriero and Yann LeCun, 2025, preprint, [arXiv:2511.08544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08544). Invariance/prediction on embedding vectors plus SIGReg, a random-projection Gaussianity regularizer; explicitly avoids EMA and stop-gradient in its recipe. Action: no in the basic image setup. ImageNet representation evidence and theory under stated assumptions. **Us:** a controlled alternative anti-collapse baseline, not evidence that a Gaussian representation must capture grasping.

7. **VICReg — “Variance-Invariance-Covariance Regularization for Self-Supervised Learning.”** Adrien Bardes, Jean Ponce and Yann LeCun, 2021/2022, ICLR 2022, [arXiv:2105.04906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.04906). View-level invariance, per-dimension variance floor and covariance penalty oppose constant and redundant features; action: no. Image SSL evaluation. **Us:** useful diagnostics and optional regularizer, but variance alone cannot ensure the right state variables survive.

8. **“How JEPA Avoids Noisy Features: The Implicit Bias of Deep Linear Self Distillation Networks.”** Etai Littwin et al., 2024, NeurIPS 2024, [arXiv:2407.03475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03475). Analyzes feature selection rather than proposing an action model or new collapse mechanism. In a restricted deep-linear, simultaneously diagonalizable setting, latent prediction prioritizes high regression-coefficient features; in a corresponding example predictable low-variance slow features are favored. **Us:** an instructive bias, not a theorem that nonlinear JEPA learns semantics or that low variance equals physical relevance.

9. **“When Does LeJEPA Learn a World Model?”** David Klindt, Yann LeCun and Randall Balestriero, 2026, preprint, [arXiv:2605.26379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26379). Studies vector embeddings with alignment and SIGReg; under a specified stationary additive-noise world class establishes Gaussian latent linear identifiability and related planning implications. Not a general action-conditioned robot guarantee. **Us:** non-collapse and identifiability are different claims; occlusion, multimodal contacts and finite demos do not satisfy the theorem automatically.

10. **“Proving and Improving the Platonic Representation Hypothesis with JEPAs.”** Randall Balestriero and Yann LeCun, 2026, preprint, [arXiv:2610.39302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39302), submitted 29 October. Separately trained modality embeddings can align up to rotation under the paper's “good views” assumptions; examines image/text alignment, not action dynamics or a robot collapse experiment. Tests 145k-caption through 940M-image text settings. **Us:** view selection and paired information matter; this does not prove vision alone identifies contact state or physical laws.

11. **LeWorldModel — “Stable End-to-End Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture from Pixels.”** Lucas Maes, Quentin Le Lidec, Damien Scieur, Yann LeCun and Randall Balestriero, 2026, preprint, [arXiv:2603.19312](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19312). Compact action-conditioned next **embedding** prediction with SIGReg, no EMA teacher or pixel decoder; approximately 15M parameters, single GPU in a few hours in the reported settings. Visual control and physical probes. **Us:** valuable small-model baseline, though training from scratch on 84 robot demonstrations is unproven.

12. **H-JEPA — “End-to-End Learning of Hierarchical World Models for Visual Planning.”** Wancong Zhang, Basile Terver, Michael Rabbat, Yann LeCun and Randall Balestriero, 2026, preprint, [arXiv:2610.06805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06805). Action-conditioned levels predict progressively farther in **distinct learned latent spaces**, regularized with SIGReg; top-down subgoals. Four simulated tasks and offline DROID analysis; reports Visual AntMaze success 18%→73% versus a flat model. **Us:** slow task states may be useful, but hierarchy is an experiment, not evidence that long-horizon detail is automatically unnecessary for grasping.

13. **“Hierarchical Planning with Latent World Models” (HWM).** Wancong Zhang et al., 2026, preprint, [arXiv:2604.03208](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03208). Action-conditioned long/short horizon predictors in a **shared latent**, macro-actions and top-down subgoals; uses pretrained latent features rather than a new general anti-collapse theorem. Real Franka goal-image pick/place and simulated planning; reported 70% on cup and 60% on box versus 0% flat baseline in its evaluation. **Us:** use nearby phase targets; real robot data and strong pretrained backbone far exceed 84 demos.

14. **“Semigroup-JEPA: Latent Dynamics Consistency for Zero-Shot Physics Generalization.”** Andy Zeyi Liu, Haoran Sun, Lucas Baker, Randall Balestriero and John Sous, 2026, preprint, [arXiv:2609.10464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10464). Conditions latent dynamics on a physics parameter and backpropagates autoregressive rollout loss into encoder, building on LeWM/SIGReg; action/parameter conditioned. Controlled gravity shifts and 3D robotic datasets; reports improved errors/control relative to DINO-WM. **Us:** tests long rollouts and representation adaptation, but known physics parameters and controlled simulation are not your setting.

15. **“Adaptive Latent Capacity for World Models.”** Idan Achituve, Lior Dikstein, Idit Diamant, Arnon Netzer and Hai Victor Habi, 2026, preprint, [arXiv:2609.32921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32921). ALeWM predicts full future embedding from sampled learned prefixes; MixSIGReg enforces a prefix-dependent mixture prior. Action-conditioned visual control and controlled dynamical tests. **Us:** a principled variable-capacity ablation, but tuning a new prior on a small batch of correlated robot clips is risky.

16. **CF-JEPA — “Improving Robustness of JEPA World Models via Controllability Factorization.”** Morgan Byrd, Robert Wright and Sehoon Ha, 2026, preprint, [arXiv:2610.00727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00727). Splits action-controllable from uncontrollable latent features; action-conditioned model and factorization/regularization prevent the collapse seen under their moving distractors. 2D/3D control and simulated robot. **Us:** test backgrounds explicitly; retain uncontrollable obstacles and bin geometry, which remain causally relevant despite not being directly actuated.

17. **“A Lightweight Library for Energy-Based Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures” (EB-JEPA).** Basile Terver et al., 2026, preprint, [arXiv:2602.03604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03604). Modular representation-space energy/prediction examples, including action-conditioned world models, with anti-collapse components studied in ablation; CIFAR, Moving MNIST and Two Rooms. Examples designed for single GPUs in hours. **Us:** practical reference for objective ablations, not evidence that a point predictor represents multimodal futures.

### Planning, target granularity and physical evidence

18. **DINO-WM — “World Models on Pre-trained Visual Features enable Zero-shot Planning.”** Gaoyue Zhou, Hengkai Pan, Yann LeCun and Lerrel Pinto, 2024, preprint, [arXiv:2411.04983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04983). Frozen pretrained DINOv2 **spatial patch features** are action-conditioned prediction targets; pretrained features anchor collapse prevention rather than learning the encoder from the small planning dataset. CEM optimizes action sequences against image-goal latent distance across six simulated environments, including pushing. **Us:** strongest nearby baseline for a fixed-token and equal-compute comparison; not a real dual-arm 84-demo demonstration.

19. **PLDM — “Learning from Reward-Free Offline Data: A Case for Planning with Latent Dynamics Models.”** Vlad Sobal, Wancong Zhang, Kyunghyun Cho, Randall Balestriero, Tim G. J. Rudner and Yann LeCun, 2025, preprint, [arXiv:2502.14819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14819). Action-conditioned latent transition and planning on reward-free offline trajectories; non-collapse aided by representation regularization and inverse-dynamics supervision. Studies diverse dataset quality and generalization in controlled environments. **Us:** compare behavior cloning versus model-based reranking on identical demonstrations; neither result transfers automatically to small, narrow real datasets.

20. **“What Drives Success in Physical Planning with Joint-Embedding Predictive World Models?”** Basile Terver, Tsung-Yen Yang, Jean Ponce, Adrien Bardes and Yann LeCun, 2025/2026, TMLR 2026, [arXiv:2512.24497](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24497). Compares JEPA-WM architecture, loss and planning choices for action-conditioned latent prediction; target and anti-collapse depend on the variant. Simulation and real robotic data; proposed combination outperforms DINO-WM and V-JEPA 2-AC in the tested settings. **Us:** evaluate the whole representation–predictor–planner stack, not latent MSE alone.

21. **“The Planning Limits of Latent World Models.”** Ali Alrasheed, Basim Azam and Naveed Akhtar, 2026, preprint, [arXiv:2609.39235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39235). Frozen pretrained feature backbones with action-conditioned latent prediction; frozen features anchor representation. Meta-World and BridgeData V2; for a five-step imagined horizon, useful target range was about 5–10 steps. Nearby expert subgoals substantially helped; ranking eight VLA proposals improved success 65%→77% across 16 tasks. **Us:** start with short-horizon flow-policy candidate ranking and closed-loop feedback, not a distant terminal-image loss.

22. **TD-MPC2 — “Scalable, Robust World Models for Continuous Control.”** Nicklas Hansen, Hao Su and Xiaolong Wang, 2023/2024, ICLR 2024, [arXiv:2310.16828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16828). Decoder-free, action-conditioned compact latent dynamics with reward/value/policy learning and sampling-based MPC; task reward/value supervision and consistency anchor representation rather than a JEPA-only anti-collapse guarantee. 104 online RL tasks, up to 317M parameters across 80 tasks. **Us:** efficient task-directed latent planning, but online reward learning is not directly comparable to 84 reward-free demos.

23. **DreamingV2 — “Reinforcement Learning with Discrete World Models without Reconstruction.”** Masashi Okada and Tadahiro Taniguchi, 2022, preprint, [arXiv:2203.00494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00494). Stochastic discrete action-conditioned latent states, contrastive reconstruction-free training, and imagined policy learning; contrastive negatives oppose trivial collapse. Five simulated 3D arm tasks. **Us:** useful precedent for discrete/multimodal belief, but RL experience differs radically from your offline data.

24. **MuDreamer — “Learning Predictive World Models without Reconstruction.”** Maxime Burchi and Radu Timofte, 2024, preprint, [arXiv:2405.15083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15083). DreamerV3-derived stochastic action-conditioned model, replacing image reconstruction with value and previous-action prediction; batch normalization is reported crucial to avoid collapse. Visual control with video distractions and Atari100k. **Us:** direct DreamerV3 decoder-free comparison; value supervision and online RL are extra signals unavailable by default here.

25. **“Navigation World Models.”** Amir Bar, Gaoyue Zhou, Danny Tran, Trevor Darrell and Yann LeCun, 2024, preprint, [arXiv:2412.03572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03572). Action-conditioned **video generation** by a 1B-parameter diffusion transformer; generative denoising objective rather than JEPA collapse control. Egocentric human/robot video and trajectory ranking or planning. **Us:** generative counterexample and proposal-ranking idea; photorealistic generation and navigation data scale are expensive and not necessary for this task.

26. **“Toward Physically Grounded JEPA World Models for Goal-Conditioned Robotic Planning.”** Muyuan Liu, Yue Huang, Zheng Liang and Xiang Gao, 2026, preprint, [arXiv:2609.03565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03565). Action-conditioned latent prediction plus inverse-dynamics and physical state-alignment heads; these auxiliary constraints supplement anti-collapse and anchor state. Four benchmark tasks, reporting better planning with state alignment than inverse dynamics alone. **Us:** use robot proprioception and reliable relative geometry, while distinguishing actual measurements from mask-derived noisy labels.

27. **“Spectral-Target Physical Latent Structuring for JEPA-Style World Models.”** Penghao Zhu, Salvatore Penachio, Kaustav Mukherjee and Aneesh Jonelagadda, 2026, preprint, [arXiv:2609.04264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04264). LeWM/SIGReg action-conditioned latent model plus a Fourier auxiliary head to encourage physically structured features. Controlled dynamical planning tests and low-data experiments show benefits. **Us:** latent variance can be healthy while physical information is absent; first measure probes before importing a specific Fourier prior.

28. **IntPhys 2 — “Benchmarking Intuitive Physics Understanding In Complex Synthetic Environments.”** Florian Bordes, Quentin Garrido, Justine T. Kao, Adina Williams, Michael Rabbat and Emmanuel Dupoux, 2025, preprint, [arXiv:2506.09849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09849). A **benchmark**, not a world model: video violation-of-expectation tests for permanence, immutability, continuity and solidity; no action conditioning or collapse objective. Most evaluated models near 50% versus near-perfect humans on complex scenes. **Us:** train/test simple interventions and occlusion explicitly; benchmark success is not proof of reliable physical control.

29. **“Do Video Foundation Models Understand Intuitive Physics? A Layerwise Probing Analysis.”** Samuele Punzo, Niccolò Caselli, Ippokratis Pantelidis, Francesco Massafra, Salvatore Lo Sardo and Mohammadreza Salehi, 2026, preprint, [arXiv:2606.09646](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09646). Frozen V-JEPA, VideoMAE and diffusion features tested with layerwise and temporal probes on IntPhys2 and MVP; no action-conditioned training or new collapse mechanism. V-JEPA strongest overall in this evaluation, but results depend on layer and probe and temporal shuffling degrades them. **Us:** use held-out, simple probes and matched controls; accessible information is not necessarily used by the policy.

30. **FF-JEPA — “Long-Horizon Planning in World Models with Latent Planners.”** Sergi Masip, Jonathan Swinnen, Yutong Hu, Renaud Detry and Tinne Tuytelaars, 2026, preprint, [arXiv:2606.09311](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09311). Action-conditioned forward dynamics plus action-free latent subgoal predictor; pretrained/regularized JEPA representation is the anchor, with no independent collapse guarantee claimed here. Preliminary PushT results. **Us:** phase-level subgoals are promising, but early single-domain evidence does not justify a full hierarchy yet.

## Synthesis: what should be predicted?

A latent feature is still detail if it responds to lighting, background, reflections, mask edges or camera jitter and the loss pays heavily to reproduce those changes. Conversely, precise hand–bottle clearance or bottle rim orientation can be **fine detail that is essential**. “Predict what is predictable” does not mean “predict only slowly changing features”: a moving hand and a contact transition can be both fast and decisive. With a deterministic squared-error predictor, genuinely multimodal futures can average; a small error may reflect an easy static scene or teacher drift rather than an adequate dynamics model. A latent representation can encode nuisances even with no image decoder.

A reasonable first **experimental starting objective**, not a literature-derived optimum, is

`L = 1.0 L_flow + 0.5 L_object + 0.25 L_grid + 0.25 L_geometry + 0.1 L_cf`.

Define each term as a **mean over valid examples, dimensions and appropriate tokens**, normalized against a fixed baseline or monitored by encoder/predictor gradient norms. The units of raw flow-matching, cosine/Huber, BCE and contrastive losses are incomparable: coefficients are starting points only, not evidence that the terms contribute in those proportions. Keep the exact existing objective as baseline and sweep one weight at a time. `L_object` is matched, visible/confident object-token prediction, balanced by object and phase rather than number of pixels. `L_grid` is spatial latent prediction, balanced separately over foreground/contact neighborhood and background; start with foreground:background weights 3:1 within that term, normalize each region independently and clip if background is absent. Preserve a low-weight context term so the bin and obstacles survive. `L_geometry` combines normalized centroid/box or relative pose deltas and gripper state; use mask BCE/Dice only on reliable visible masks, weight it lower than centroid/contact geometry and ablate it separately. If labels are noisy, do not silently promote them to ground truth. `L_cf` uses valid, action-distinguishable negatives, not arbitrary swapped actions treated as true physical futures. The policy remains trained on demonstrated action chunks; keep a current image/proprioception bypass.

Do not assume nine object tokens outweigh 240 grid tokens. With a mean over all tokens, 240/249 ≈ 96.4% of token contributions come from the grid **if dimensions, per-token weights and errors are equal**; actual gradient contribution can differ greatly. Log both term-level encoder gradient norms and per-region loss; inspect action sensitivity and task performance before resizing. Likewise a mask head can dominate merely by summing many pixels. Use equal-example/region reduction and report how absent objects and visibility are handled. Do not claim a measured waste until ablations support it.

For multiscale experiments retain 12×20 inputs to the perception/policy branch but compare dynamics outputs at 12×20, 6×10 and a small set of object/ROI tokens under matched parameter count, training steps and GPU time. For bottle and both hands add higher-resolution cropped or ROI tokens, with scene context and bin retained. Pool **after** checking that thin grippers, rim, bottle orientation and short distances are not aliased. A centroid/box alone is insufficient to represent surface contact, depth, occlusion or the difference between two grasps. Model absent/occluded tokens with a visibility flag and track IDs or explicit matching, not zero-padding interpreted as an actual object. Identity switches and stale SAM masks can create false motion.

+5 and +10 correspond to different levels of action uncertainty; do not weight each future patch identically across horizons. Begin with the existing two horizons and measure each separately. If longer-horizon patch prediction is hard but object placement remains predictable, give long horizons a smaller grid weight and preserve coarse object/phase prediction; keep short-horizon local geometry. The 3–6 chunk rollout (2–4 s) may extend past the trustworthy regime: measure error and action ranking per chunk and stop or reobserve when unreliable. Nearby subgoals and receding-horizon control are better supported than simply increasing rollout depth. HWM uses a shared space; H-JEPA learns separate spaces—do not conflate the two.

Uncertainty is not permission to ignore contact: compare a deterministic predictor with a small ensemble or a modest heteroscedastic head, separately reporting calibration on held-out episodes. Likelihood models can inflate predicted variance to avoid hard loss; regularize variance, bound it, and test coverage versus actual error. For multiple valid grasp/contact outcomes, an ensemble or small mixture may help only if data contain repeated diverse outcomes. An energy-based score or latent-variable JEPA could represent multiple compatible futures in principle, but requires negative/sampling design and calibration; EB-JEPA's name alone does not establish calibrated multimodal inference. With 84 demos, first favor empirical short-horizon error and rejection of unsupported actions over training a large stochastic video generator.

## Measuring capacity waste, collapse and control relevance

Use episode-level splits, never overlapping frames or chunks from the same demonstration across splits; where possible hold out bottle instances, initial layouts, lighting or recording sessions separately. Report uncertainty over episodes and seeds; 84 trajectories are not 84 independent examples for every phase and rare failures may be absent. Leave a final test set untouched while choosing weights.

1. **Baselines:** compare persistence (`ẑ(t+h)=z(t)`), action-shuffled or zero-action dynamics, the existing model, and a policy-only flow head with the same encoder, compute and augmentations. Report +5/+10 and each autoregressive chunk separately, plus end-effector and bottle/hand/bin region metrics. A model that beats persistence only on background or whose shuffled-action score is unchanged is not demonstrating useful action dynamics.
2. **Action discrimination:** on held-out real interventions, compare predicted outcomes or goal ranking for actual versus *physically executed* alternate action chunks from similar initial states. Swapping recorded actions produces a diagnostic, not a known counterfactual label. If feasible collect matched safe short interventions across approach, contact and release, including both arms, and evaluate action ranking with true observed outcomes. Measure change in predictions under action perturbation relative to variation from initial state; check whether the correct arm influences the correct region. A contrastive objective can exploit state/phase shortcuts, false negatives and dataset artifacts.
3. **Allocation:** measure grid/object/background loss and gradient norms, runtime and memory. Correlate per-region errors with held-out grasp/place success, object position errors and action ranking. Perform object-only, grid-only, mixed, background-downweighted and equal-compute token-count ablations. If removing grid detail barely changes real success or action ranking while reducing compute, that is evidence of waste. If spatial ablation harms grasp even when object-token error improves, the detail was useful.
4. **Information and robustness:** freeze the encoder and fit small probes for bottle/hand positions, relative displacement, velocity, gripper opening, contact proxies, identity, visibility, phase and whether the bottle is in the bin. Use held-out episodes; compare to a frozen DINOv2 backbone, random features and persistence. Stress-test lighting, backgrounds, distractors, partial occlusion and mask corruption while maintaining task geometry; do not call a changed background irrelevant if it changes an obstacle. Probe accuracy reveals information accessibility, not causal use; complement it with region occlusion and controlled interventions.
5. **Representation health:** track target and online per-dimension standard deviations, covariance spectrum/effective rank, pairwise feature distances and cosine similarity across **different episodes and phases**, separately for object, foreground and background. Also track transition differences and action-conditioned output diversity. EMA may delay collapse rather than preclude it; high global rank may come entirely from background, identity or time. Guard against representation drift by evaluating a frozen reference encoder and comparing checkpoints on the same held-out frames. With small batches, covariance/SIGReg estimates are noisy and temporally correlated; sample across episodes, report uncertainty, and avoid enforcing variance on constant or absent objects.
6. **Task metrics:** report phase-specific grasp, lift, placement and complete success, failure categories and time, with number of trials and confidence intervals. Compare teacher-forced vs free-running prediction, and close the loop at each chunk. Evaluate unseen initial poses and difficult occlusions, not only reconstruction of successful training demonstrations. A visually plausible latent rollout, low aggregate loss or good policy imitation error alone does not prove that a planner can select better actions.

## Five ranked next directions

1. **Instrument and rebalance the existing model.** Add the diagnostics above, normalize per head/region and perform the baseline/weight ablations before replacing architecture. Cost: low to moderate, one GPU and repeated small runs. Risk: noisy estimates from 84 demos; use episode bootstrap and phase counts. Success: identify which losses improve action ranking and closed-loop phase success, not merely total loss.
2. **Ground short-horizon action effects in measured geometry.** Keep object tokens, grid context and proprioception; add/validate relative gripper–bottle, bottle–bin, both-arm and visibility/velocity heads. Start with existing reliable labels, and collect targeted varied short interventions if feasible. Cost: low to moderate labeling/data-collection plus small heads. Risk: mask errors, ambiguous depth/contact and shortcut inverse dynamics; assess with held-out alternate actions and label confidence. This gives a practical definition of “physical laws” as verified local action–effect relations rather than unconstrained symbolic law discovery.
3. **Test mixed spatial/object granularity at equal compute.** Compare 12×20, 6×10 and ROI-plus-object prediction with identical policy input resolution, and vary only foreground/background weights first. Cost: moderate, several single-GPU experiments. Risk: losing fine contact, bin boundaries or small gripper cues; require phase-specific evaluation and preserve a high-resolution perception bypass. Dense V-JEPA 2.1 and DINO-WM make an object-only replacement premature.
4. **Use the flow policy as a proposal distribution and the model as a short-horizon critic.** Sample a small number of action chunks, score predicted object/geometry progress and risk, execute one and reobserve. Compare random selection and direct flow policy with identical candidate budget; include latency and action-ranking calibration. Cost: moderate inference and targeted outcome labels, likely feasible on one GPU; risk: model exploitation/OOD proposals, ambiguous scoring and unsafe exploratory behavior. Constrain candidate actions to demonstrated support and robot limits, and validate offline then under appropriate operational controls. Contrast with GAWM: action-free forecasts are useful policy features but cannot rank interventions.
5. **Add temporal abstraction only after short-horizon tests pass.** Define or learn nearby phase subgoals (approach, grasp, lift, above-bin, release); compare a simple phase-conditioned head to a two-timescale latent predictor. Use longer horizons mainly for compact object/phase outcomes and short horizons for local geometry. Cost: moderate to high, including phase annotation or reliable weak labels; risk: compounding errors, degenerate phase shortcuts and too few diverse demonstrations. H-JEPA/HWM are motivation, not proof a hierarchical model will train on this dataset. Scale data and counterfactual coverage before a large end-to-end hierarchy or generative simulator.

The strongest recommendation is therefore empirical: keep the current useful spatial representation available, spend less loss and rollout budget on background fidelity where ablations show it has no control value, and invest first in measured action-conditioned contact and object motion. There is no published result establishing the optimal token count or loss weights for this robot and data regime.
